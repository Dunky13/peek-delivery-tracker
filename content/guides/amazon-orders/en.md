To track an Amazon order, open Your Orders, pick the order and select Track package: Amazon shows the carrier, the tracking number and every update there. Who helps when it goes wrong depends on who shipped it. Amazon looks after its own orders and Fulfilled by Amazon ones, while a seller who dispatches its own orders picks the carrier and answers for the delivery.

## Who ships my Amazon order: Amazon or a third-party seller?

Look at the "Sold by" and "Ships from" lines under the Buy Now button, or at the seller name in Your Orders. amazon.co.uk says "Dispatches from" instead of "Ships from".

| What the page says | What it means | Who helps with delivery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sold by Amazon, Ships from Amazon | Amazon's own stock | Amazon |
| Sold by a seller, Ships from Amazon | Fulfilled by Amazon: the seller's stock, packed and sent from an Amazon warehouse | Amazon |
| Sold by and Ships from the same seller | The seller ships it with its own carrier | The seller first |

Amazon.com.au shows Fulfilled by Amazon items as "Sold by \[Seller Name\] and Fulfilled by Amazon". Amazon.ca writes "Ships from and Sold by \[SELLER NAME\]" for a seller's own shipments.

Two more cases:

- **Delivery Managed by Amazon** (amazon.co.uk): the seller sells and dispatches the item, but Amazon manages the delivery and "may engage third-party carriers". You track it in Your Orders.
- **Amazon Global Store**: imported products, shown with the full price including import duties, taxes and fees. They can take longer: Amazon.com.au lists "international customs procedures" among the reasons for late Global Store orders. More in [parcels held at customs](guide:customs).

Amazon.co.uk adds that sellers handle customer service for orders they ship, "unless the order is eligible for Amazon Prime". A seller's item delivered with Prime goes to Amazon too.

## Which carrier delivers my Amazon parcel?

Track package names it. Amazon's own help says it plainly: "There might be a different carrier for each package." When Amazon is the carrier, that's [Amazon Logistics](carrier:amazon-logistics), its own delivery network. These are the carriers Amazon lists on each site's carrier contact page:

| Country | Site | Carriers Amazon names |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US | amazon.com | Amazon, [USPS](carrier:usps), [UPS](carrier:ups), [FedEx](carrier:fedex), [DHL Express](carrier:dhl-express), plus [OnTrac](carrier:ontrac), [LSO](carrier:lso), [Landmark Global](carrier:landmark-global), Aramex, Fidelitone, Parcel Pool and TForce Final Mile |
| UK | amazon.co.uk | Amazon, [Royal Mail](carrier:royal-mail), [Evri](carrier:evri) (Amazon's own orders only), [DPD](carrier:dpd-uk), [Parcelforce](carrier:parcelforce), Yodel, UPS, FedEx, DHL, An Post, Aramex, Jersey Post |
| Ireland | amazon.ie | Amazon, [An Post](carrier:an-post), [DPD](carrier:dpd-ie), UPS, Parcelforce |
| Canada | amazon.ca | [Canada Post](carrier:canada-post), [Purolator](carrier:purolator), [Intelcom \| Dragonfly](carrier:intelcom), UPS, Day & Ross, ASL, BNI, RDL, CEVA, DHL, Pitney Bowes, Landmark |
| Australia | amazon.com.au | Amazon Logistics, [Australia Post](carrier:australia-post), StarTrack, [Aramex](carrier:aramex-au), [CouriersPlease](carrier:couriersplease), Team Global Express, DHL, FedEx, UPS and others |

A seller who ships its own orders can use any of these, or another carrier. Where exactly the number sits on each shop: [find your tracking number](guide:find-tracking-number).

## What does an Amazon tracking number look like?

There are two kinds, and an order number that is neither.

Amazon doesn't publish the format of its own numbers. These are the shapes Peek recognises as Amazon's, here with made-up digits:

:::anatomy TBA 123456789012
- TBA | Amazon letters. `TBC` and `TBM` work the same way
- 123456789012 | Twelve digits
:::

| Shape (made up) | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `TBA123456789012` | Amazon: `TBA`, `TBC` or `TBM` and 12 digits |
| `UK1234567890` | Amazon in Europe: a country code (`UK`, `GB`, `IE` and others) and 10 digits |
| `123-1234567-1234567` | Your Amazon order number: 3, 7 and 7 digits. Not a tracking number |
| Anything else | The carrier's own number, such as Royal Mail's or USPS's |

## Why did my Amazon tracking number change, or why are there two?

Usually one of these:

- **Several items, several parcels.** "If you ordered more than one thing, they may have separate delivery dates and tracking info." An item missing from a box "may have been shipped separately".
- **A partner carrier takes over.** Amazon.ca: "Some national carriers use regional carriers. In this case, your tracking information may not show on the national carrier's website." For Delivery Managed by Amazon, Amazon may hand the parcel to another carrier.
- **The seller uses its own carrier.** Then the number is that carrier's, not Amazon's.
- **Different words, same parcel.** "Every carrier uses their own language", Amazon says, so it simplifies the updates in Your Orders. Its `Delivery attempted` is USPS's `Notice left`, for example.

:::journey
- warehouse | Amazon or the seller | Several items can leave as several parcels, each with its own number
- label | Label made | No tracking until the carrier's first scan
- handover | Regional or partner carrier | May take over, sometimes with its own number
- truck | Out for delivery | "Your carrier will have the latest information"
- home | You | Can show delivered up to 48 hours early
:::

When the first carrier names the partner taking over, [Peek](/) keeps the first history, follows the partner once its own tracking shows fresh progress, and shows the partner's name and number on the card.

## Why is there no tracking for my Amazon order?

Amazon gives these reasons on its "Missing Tracking Information" page:

- **No first scan yet.** For orders sold and fulfilled by Amazon, the number appears once the carrier scans the parcel: "Until this first scan, the carrier may not acknowledge receipt of the package."
- **The seller hasn't shared it.** Seller orders show tracking only if the seller passed it to Amazon and you chose a trackable shipping option. "Marketplace sellers don't always provide us with tracking information."
- **Untracked service.** "Some shipments, such as Standard International shipments, aren't trackable."
- **It's a gift.** The person who bought it gets the tracking updates.
- **Canada's regional hubs.** On amazon.ca, "the first package scan may be on arrival at a regional hub near the destination", and sometimes not until delivery.

No Track package option on a seller order? Go to Your Orders, choose Problem with order and then Contact seller. If the estimated date has passed and nothing has changed, Amazon.ie and Amazon.ca say to wait a day or two more. Amazon.com.au adds that sellers sometimes don't pass tracking to Amazon: then "you may need to visit the website of the carrier delivering your package". When tracking shows nothing new for days: [why tracking stops updating](guide:tracking-not-updating).

## What to do if an Amazon order is late or says delivered

Amazon's statuses in Your Orders are `Arriving`, `Out for delivery`, `Delivered` and `Undeliverable`. That last one means the parcel is going back to Amazon, which refunds you automatically once it arrives. For the rest, go in this order:

:::steps
- Wait 48 hours | "Most late parcels arrive within 48 hours of the estimated delivery date." A parcel can also show delivered up to 48 hours before it arrives.
- Look around | Check where you asked it to be left, your mailbox and the neighbours. Some parcels travel through more than one carrier.
- Ask the carrier | Track package names it. Amazon's carrier contact page links each one.
- Contact the seller | If a seller sold and shipped it: Your Orders, Problem with order, Contact seller. Sellers reply within 48 hours (two business days in Ireland and Australia).
- Ask Amazon | Orders shipped by Amazon: customer service. In the US, UK and Canada, do it within 30 days of the estimated delivery date. Seller orders: the A-to-z Guarantee.
:::

For more on each case: [delivered but not received](guide:delivered-not-received) and [lost parcels](guide:lost-parcel).

### When can I claim the A-to-z Guarantee?

It covers items a third-party seller sold and shipped itself. For a parcel that never came, three days must have passed since the latest estimated delivery date (or since tracking showed it delivered, whichever is sooner), and you must have contacted the seller more than 48 hours earlier. You start it from Problem with order in Your Orders.

| Site | Seller replies within | Claim window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| amazon.com, amazon.ca | 48 hours | Last possible delivery date 90 days ago or less |
| amazon.co.uk | 48 hours | Last delivery date 90 days ago or less |
| amazon.ie | Two business days | Last possible delivery date 90 days ago or less |
| amazon.com.au | Two business days | Up to 90 days after the maximum estimated delivery date |

Global Store items go to Amazon's customer service instead, and so, on amazon.com and amazon.co.uk, do seller items delivered with Prime. On amazon.com.au, if you chose "Authority to leave in a safe place" and tracking shows it delivered, the claim isn't eligible. If the seller didn't cover customs charges you had to pay, that can be claimed too.

## Can I track an Amazon parcel outside Amazon?

It depends on the number. Paste it into [Peek](/):

- **A carrier's number** (Royal Mail, USPS, Canada Post, Australia Post…) is tracked like any other parcel.
- **An Amazon number**, or a track.amazon link: Peek first asks Amazon's public tracker. If Amazon answers, the parcel travels with [Amazon Shipping](carrier:amazon-shipping), Amazon's delivery service for other retailers and Amazon sellers, or with Multi-Channel Fulfilment, where Amazon packs orders placed on other sites, often in plain packaging. Peek then tracks it automatically.
- Otherwise it's Amazon Logistics delivering an Amazon order, and Amazon shows that only in your account. Peek says so and offers "Open my Amazon orders", which opens your order history on the Amazon site the number points to. Peek never asks for your Amazon login.
- **An order number** gets "That looks like an Amazon order number". Use the tracking number from Track package or the shipping email instead.
